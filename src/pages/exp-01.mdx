## Observations

<Obs n={1}>
**Quantization is the price of boundary conditions.** The walls demand ψ(0) = ψ(L) = 0. Only sine waves that fit a half-integer number of wavelengths into the box satisfy that, which selects a discrete set of allowed states labeled by *n* = 1, 2, 3, …. The full derivation, including the normalization that makes <em>A = √(2/L)</em>, was worked out in <WikiLink slug="lecture-00" display="the foundations lecture" />.<SideNote n={1}>Cosines fail the left boundary: cos(0) = 1, not 0. Inside an infinite well the most general solution is a sum of sine and cosine; the cosine coefficient is killed by ψ(0) = 0.</SideNote>
</Obs>

<Obs n={2}>
The energy is *E<sub>n</sub> = n² π² ℏ² / (2 m L²)*. Two scalings worth feeling: **E ∝ n²** and **E ∝ 1/L²**.<SideNote n={2}>This 1/L² scaling is essentially why atoms have a size. Confining an electron near the nucleus increases kinetic energy faster than the Coulomb attraction lowers potential energy.</SideNote>
</Obs>

<Obs n={3}>
**The node theorem.** The state *n* has exactly *n* − 1 internal zero crossings. This isn't a quirk of the square well — it holds for every 1D bound-state problem.<SideNote n={3}>The harmonic oscillator (Exp. 02, coming soon) is the next concrete example.</SideNote>
</Obs>


## Open questions

- What happens if the walls aren't infinitely high? The finite well admits *tunneling*.
- What is the time evolution of a *superposition* like ψ₁ + ψ₂? The energy levels suggest a beat frequency proportional to (E₂ − E₁).
- The momentum eigenstates of this Hamiltonian are not the energy eigenstates. What does ⟨p²⟩ look like for ψₙ?
