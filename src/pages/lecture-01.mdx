## Lets Begin!
So so far I have seen Quantum uncertainties, Normalization and expectation values, the wave function and how it relates 
to probability amplitude and probability density.

In this lecture, we now take the "particle in a box" example that i used to understand normalization and expectation values, and it explains the physics of why the wave behaves that way.

First of all it is important to note that this concept is **HIGHLY IDEALIZED !!!** . It is essentially the "Hello World" of Quantum mechanics. It feels like a math trick because its so "Fake". In this idealized "box" math we ignore other particles or outside forces, we simply define the space outside as "forbidden". It all seems "fake" and highly idealized from what i have learnt below and it got me wondering what the importance of studying it is  but apparently its importance lies in the 2 massive concepts it proves that classical mechanics cannot explain:

1. **Quantization (The "Digital" Nature of energy**) - In our everyday world, if you throw a ball, it can have any speed you want, IKph, 2 etc, but in this box, the math proves that the particle cannot have just any energy. Because the wave function must be zero at the walls and beyond, only specific vibrations can fit inside. 
   
- The lesson here is that it explains why electrons in atoms have specific "shells" or levels, rather than just floating anywhere within the atom. Its the reason for the "Quantum" in Quantum mechanics

2. **The Death of "Rest**" (Zero point energy) - idk why i love this concept, I just do😂, but inside the box if the particle just sat perfectly still, it would violate **Heisenbergs Uncertainty Principle**

- The lesson here is that the box proves that even at absolute zero temperatures, matter still has to jitter. Nothing in the universe is ever truly still.

but even though this box is a "perfected" version, the same math is used to design actual tech:
- **Quantum Dots:** These are tiny "boxes" (nanocrystals) where we trap electrons. By changing the **size of the box (\(L\))**, we change the energy levels, which changes the color of light the dot emits. This is how high-end QLED TVs create such specific colors.
- **Conductive Polymers:** We treat the backbone of long molecules like a "box" for electrons to move through, helping us understand how electricity flows through plastic or organic solar cells.
- **Color in Molecules:** The reason some dyes (like those in carrots or leaves) are specific colors is that their electrons are trapped in "boxes" created by their chemical bonds.

So even though its highly idealistic, im thinking of it like flying a plane simulator  with no wind or gravity and once you master the idealized verrsion, I add just a little leakage (tunneling) or "bumps" (potential) to understand how real world atoms actually work. So, Lets Get to it.

ok so back to particle in a box, inside the box (from 0-L), the particle moves freely, 
meaning its potential energy is zero (U=0). Outside the box, the potential energy
shoots up to infinity ($U=\infty$). Because the particle would need infinite energy to exist out there, the wave function outside the box is exactly zero ($\psi = 0$)

It is important to note that in this "particular box" model, the "box" is not made of wood
or metal but is defined by **impenetrable barriers**.

A good analogy to better understand this woulld be imagining a ball bouncing between
2 walls. If the walls are infinite, it means no matter how hard the bll hits on both walls, they
willnever move or break or allow the ball to pass. 

In physics terms, it would take **infinite work(energy)** to displace the boundary
or push the ball into the wall. By setting (U=$\infty$) outside,we  are essentially
"locking" the ball inside the barrier. It is a way of saying ***it is impossible for the ball to be anywhere else.***

I was not understanding why the wave function outside the box was zero, but i came up with the following explanation:

Well in the previous <WikiLink slug="lecture-00" display="lecture" />, I understood that a wave function $\psi(x)$ is
**a complex valued mathematical description of a quantum particle that represents
the probability amplitude of finding the said particle at a specific location**. Thuus at the walls and 
beyond, the wave function must be zero.

1. **Conservation of Energy** - In order for the particle to exist in a region where U=$/infty$, It would need an infinite amount 
of energy. Since our particle has got a finite fixed energy (Zero point energy),it simply cannot exist there.

2. **The Probability rule** - If the probabiloity of finding the particle outside is zero, the math has to reflect that. Since the 
actual probability of finding the particle is calculated by the probability density $|\psi|^2$, if the probability is zero, then $\psi$ 
itself must be zero.

Then again I found myself needing an explanation of what this potential energy U is.

However, I have learnt that in physics, it is a bit of a more formal setup. like the "particle in a box" above,

no hills or valleys (forces) pushing the particle around when inside. The key distinction for my logic iis as follows:

Mathematically, the Schrodinger equation shows that if the potential energy is much higher than the particles energy, the wave function must decay to zero. Since the potential here is infinitely high,

I love analogies so i got to thinking of this "anchoring" like a guitar string of length L. Because both ends are bolted down, you can only create vibrations
that have "nodes" (points of zero movement) at those exact spots. The waves that fit in the box must have an integral number of whole or half-wavelengths giving us this core rule

$$\lambda = \frac{2L}{n}$$

where;
L = width of the box
$\lambda$ = the wavelength (distance for one full up & down cycle)
n = the mode index (or quantum number). It represents which level the particle is in.

Breaking down the levels in the above image:

| n | Whats happening    | λ    | Why it fits                                             |
| :-: |:-------------------|:----:|:--------------------------------------------------------|
| n=1 | one big "hump"     | 2L   | Half of a full wave. Starts at 0 and ends at 0.         |
| n=2 | one full "s" shape | L    | One full wave sits perfectly between both walls.        |
| n=3 | one and a half humps | 2L/3 | Three half waves — again starts and ends at zero.     |
| n=4 | 2 full "s" shapes  | L/2  | Two full waves, fits perfectly.                         |

A very important phenomenon of QM actually comes out of this **Quantization** of Energy.

Since the particle is acting like a wave, its momentum is linked to its wavlength ($\lambda$)

- Small $\lambda$ (more humps) = High Momentum = **High energy**
- Large $\lambda$ (fewerr humps) = Low momentum =**Low energy**

Because the walls "force" the wave to have specific wavelengths, like (L) 0r (2L/3), the particle is
forced to also have specific energy levels. It literally cannot vibrate at a wavelength like (1.15L)
because it would not hit zero ath the right hand wall, it would "crash" into  the infinite potential.

***The "zero point" connection***

Notice that there is no (n=0). if n=0, the wavelenth would be infinite (a flat line), which would mean that the particle
has got zero energy and zero motion. But a flat line at zero also means that the **particle does not exist!** This proves why the 
particle ought to have at least n=1 (a "jiggle") in order for it to stay inside the box.
